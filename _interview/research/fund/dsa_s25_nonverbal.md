# Non-verbal Reasoning

Non-verbal items give you *figures*, not words: a pattern is transformed step by step and you predict, complete,
or classify it. They appear on hardware/aptitude OAs and the visual-reasoning portion of many general
assessments. The whole skill is spotting **one consistent transformation** — a rotation, a reflection, a change
in count, or a shift in position — and applying it.

::: keypoint
**The four transformations that explain almost every figure item.** (1) **Rotation** — the shape turns by a
fixed angle each step (e.g. $45^\circ$ or $90^\circ$), always the same direction. (2) **Reflection** — the shape
flips across a line (a *mirror* image flips left↔right; a *water* image flips top↔bottom). (3) **Count change**
— dots, sides, or lines increase/decrease by a fixed amount. (4) **Position shift** — an element moves a fixed
step around the figure. Test these four in order and one will fit.
:::

::: trap
A **reflection is not a rotation.** A chiral shape (one with no symmetry) that has been flipped can *never* be
recreated by turning the original — this is the single most common "odd one out" and "which is the mirror image"
trick. Check handedness, not just orientation.
:::

## 1. Figure Series

A series shows several figures in order; you pick the one that continues the pattern.

![Series: an arrow turning 45 degrees clockwise each step; choose the next figure](figs/s25_series_rotation.png)

**Q1.** Which option continues the series above?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** The arrow rotates $45^\circ$ **clockwise** each step: North → North-East → East → South-East. One more
$45^\circ$ clockwise turn points it **South (straight down)**, which is option (a). Options (c) up and (d) right
are earlier frames; (b) South-West overshoots by $45^\circ$.

**Answer:** Option (a)

![Series: regular polygons gaining one side each step; choose the next figure](figs/s25_series_polygon.png)

**Q2.** Which option continues this series?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** The number of sides grows by one: triangle ($3$) → square ($4$) → pentagon ($5$) → hexagon ($6$). The
next figure must have **seven** sides (a heptagon), which is option (c). Option (a) is a hexagon (repeat), (b) an
octagon (skips one), (d) a pentagon.

**Answer:** Option (c)

::: keypoint
When a series looks confusing, count something numeric — sides, dots, line segments, or the rotation angle — and
check whether it forms an arithmetic step. A steady $+1$ or fixed-angle turn resolves most series instantly.
:::

## 2. Figure Analogy

An analogy gives a pair "A is to B" and asks you to complete "C is to ?" using the **same** transformation.

![Analogy: A to B is a 90-degree clockwise turn; apply the same to C](figs/s25_analogy_rotation.png)

**Q3.** A is to B as C is to ?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** From A to B the arrow turns $90^\circ$ **clockwise** (up → right). Apply the identical turn to C,
which points **left**: a $90^\circ$ clockwise turn takes left → **up**, i.e. option (b). Option (d) down is the
result of turning the *wrong* way ($90^\circ$ anticlockwise).

**Answer:** Option (b)

## 3. Classification (Odd One Out)

Three figures share a hidden rule; one breaks it.

![Classification: three figures are rotations of one shape and one is a reflection](figs/s25_classification.png)

**Q4.** Which figure is the odd one out?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** Options (a), (b) and (d) are the **same flag rotated** ($0^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$) — the flag
always sits on the clockwise side of the pole. Option (c) has the flag on the **opposite** side: it is a
*reflection*, which cannot be produced by any rotation of the others. So (c) is the odd one out.

**Answer:** Option (c)

::: trap
Do not pick the figure that merely "looks tilted differently" — a $90^\circ$-rotated figure still belongs to the
group. The outsider is the one whose **handedness** differs (the mirrored one).
:::

## 4. Mirror & Water Images

A **mirror image** is the reflection across a vertical line (left↔right); a **water image** is the reflection
across a horizontal line at the base (top↔bottom).

![Mirror image: pick the left-right reflection of the given flag](figs/s25_mirror.png)

**Q5.** Which option is the correct mirror image of the given figure?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** A mirror flips left↔right only. The flag, which points **right** with its base at the bottom, becomes
a flag pointing **left** with the base still at the bottom — option (b). Option (a) is unchanged, (c) is rotated
$180^\circ$, and (d) is the water (top↔bottom) image.

**Answer:** Option (b)

![Water image: pick the top-bottom reflection of the given flag](figs/s25_water.png)

**Q6.** Which option is the correct water image of the given figure?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** A water image flips top↔bottom while keeping left↔right. The base moves to the **top** and the flag
drops to the **bottom but still points right** — option (c). The trap is option (b), a $180^\circ$ rotation, where
the flag wrongly points left.

**Answer:** Option (c)

::: keypoint
Quick test for mirror vs. water: in a **mirror** image, anything pointing left now points right (up/down
unchanged); in a **water** image, up/down swap (left/right unchanged). Track one directional feature and you
never confuse them with a rotation.
:::

## 5. Counting Figures

You are given one composite figure and must count how many of a shape it contains — count **systematically** by
size, never at random.

![A square with both diagonals drawn](figs/s25_count_triangles.png)

**Q7.** How many triangles are in the figure above?
(a) $4$ (b) $6$ (c) $8$ (d) $10$

**Solution:** Count by size. **Small** triangles (a side of the square with the centre): $4$ — top, bottom, left,
right. **Large** triangles (half the square, formed by one diagonal): $4$ — the two diagonals cut the square into
these four half-squares. Total $= 4 + 4 = 8$.

**Answer:** $8$ (c)

![A square divided into four equal smaller squares by its mid-lines](figs/s25_count_squares.png)

**Q8.** How many **squares** are in this figure?
(a) $4$ (b) $5$ (c) $6$ (d) $8$

**Solution:** $4$ small squares $+ 1$ large outer square $= 5$.

**Answer:** $5$ (b)

**Q9.** In the same figure, how many **rectangles** (including squares) are there in total?
(a) $6$ (b) $8$ (c) $9$ (d) $11$

**Solution:** A rectangle is fixed by choosing $2$ of the $3$ vertical lines and $2$ of the $3$ horizontal lines:
$\binom{3}{2}\times\binom{3}{2} = 3\times 3 = 9$.

**Answer:** $9$ (c)

::: keypoint
For a grid, don't hunt visually — use the counting rule. The number of rectangles in an $m\times n$ grid of cells
is $\binom{m+1}{2}\binom{n+1}{2}$. For the $2\times 2$ grid that is $\binom{3}{2}^2 = 9$.
:::

![A triangle divided into four smaller triangles by joining the midpoints of its sides](figs/s25_matchstick.png)

**Q10.** How many triangles are in the figure above?
(a) $4$ (b) $5$ (c) $6$ (d) $8$

**Solution:** Count by size. **Small** triangles: $4$ — three point up and one (the middle) points down. **Large**: $1$ — the whole outer triangle. Total $= 4 + 1 = 5$. The usual slips are missing the inverted middle triangle or the big outer one.

**Answer:** $5$ (b)

## 6. Paper Folding & Punching

A sheet is folded, one or more holes are punched through all layers, and you choose how it looks unfolded — the
punched holes reappear **mirrored across every fold line**.

![A square sheet folded right-onto-left, one hole punched, to be unfolded](figs/s25_paperfold.png)

**Q11.** After unfolding, which option shows the correct hole pattern?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** The punch goes through both layers, so on unfolding the hole appears at its original spot **and** at
its mirror position across the vertical fold. That gives **two holes symmetric about the centre line** — option
(b). Option (a) forgets the second layer; (c) mirrors across the wrong (horizontal) line; (d) invents extra holes.

**Answer:** Option (b)

::: interview
Hardware and general-aptitude OAs use these to test spatial working memory under time pressure — a minute or less
per figure. The reliable method is mechanical, not artistic: name the single transformation (rotate / reflect /
count / shift), apply it once, and for folding remember that **each fold doubles the holes** by reflecting them
across that crease.
:::

## 7. Figure Matrix (Pattern Completion)

A $3\times 3$ grid follows a rule across its rows and down its columns; one cell is blank and you supply it. This
is the Raven's-matrix format used on many inductive-reasoning tests (SHL, AMCAT, CoCubes).

![A 3 by 3 matrix of dot patterns with the bottom-right cell missing](figs/s25_matrix.png)

**Q12.** Which option completes the matrix?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** The dot count rises by one moving right and moving down: the rows read $1,2,3$ / $2,3,4$ / $3,4,?$.
The missing cell continues the pattern to $5$ dots — option (b).

**Answer:** Option (b)

## 8. Embedded Figures

You are given a target shape and must find the answer figure that contains it hidden inside.

![A target chevron shape and four candidate figures](figs/s25_embedded.png)

**Q13.** Which option contains the target figure as a part of it?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** The target is an upward "$\wedge$" (two strokes meeting at a peak). Only the triangle in option (a)
contains that exact pair of strokes — they are its two upper sides. Option (d) is an inverted "$\vee$" (a
reflection, not the same figure); (b) and (c) contain no such peak.

**Answer:** Option (a)

::: trap
An embedded figure must appear at the **same size, orientation, and proportion**. A rotated or mirror-flipped
copy (like the inverted chevron in (d)) does not count — that is the standard distractor.
:::

## 9. Dot Situation

A dot sits in a region defined by *which shapes overlap there*; find the option that offers the same region.

![Three overlapping shapes with a dot, and four options](figs/s25_dot.png)

**Q14.** In which option can the dot occupy the same region as in the given figure — inside the triangle and the
circle, but outside the square?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** Track the three memberships. Option (a) puts the dot inside the square; (b) puts it outside the
circle; (d) puts it outside the triangle. Only option (c) has the dot inside the triangle **and** the circle
while staying outside the square.

**Answer:** Option (c)

## 10. Grouping of Identical Figures

Sort a set of figures into groups that share a hidden property.

![Six figures: triangle, circle, square, semicircle, pentagon, ellipse](figs/s25_grouping.png)

**Q15.** Split the six figures into two groups of three.
(a) $(1,3,5)$ and $(2,4,6)$
(b) $(1,2,3)$ and $(4,5,6)$
(c) $(1,2,4)$ and $(3,5,6)$
(d) $(1,4,5)$ and $(2,3,6)$

**Solution:** The dividing property is *straight-sided vs. curved*. Figures $1$ (triangle), $3$ (square) and $5$
(pentagon) are made only of straight edges; $2$ (circle), $4$ (semicircle) and $6$ (ellipse) each contain a curve.

**Answer:** $(1,3,5)$ and $(2,4,6)$ — option (a)

## 11. Rule Detection

A rule is stated; pick the figure that obeys it.

![Four figures, each a polygon with some dots inside](figs/s25_rule.png)

**Q16.** Which figure satisfies the rule "the number of dots inside equals the number of sides"?
(a) (b) (c) (d) — as shown

**Solution:** Check each: (a) triangle — $3$ sides, $3$ dots, matches; (b) square — $4$ sides, $3$ dots, no; (c)
pentagon — $5$ sides, $4$ dots, no; (d) triangle — $3$ sides, $2$ dots, no.

**Answer:** Option (a)
