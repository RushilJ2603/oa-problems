# Mensuration, Geometry & Coordinate Geometry

::: definition
**Geometry & Mensuration Formula Sheet**

- **Triangle:** Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times b \times h$. Heron's: $\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$ where $s = \frac{a+b+c}{2}$.
- **Right Triangle:** Pythagoras $a^{2} + b^{2} = c^{2}$.
- **Circle:** Area $= \pi r^{2}$, Circumference $= 2\pi r$.
- **Quadrilaterals:** Rectangle Area $= l \times w$, Square $= a^{2}$, Parallelogram $= b \times h$, Trapezium $= \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$.
- **3D Surface Area & Volume:**
  - Cube: $V = a^{3}$, SA $= 6a^{2}$.
  - Cuboid: $V = lwh$, SA $= 2(lw + wh + hl)$.
  - Cylinder: $V = \pi r^{2} h$, Curved SA $= 2\pi rh$, Total SA $= 2\pi r(r+h)$.
  - Cone: $V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^{2} h$, Curved SA $= \pi r l$ where $l = \sqrt{r^{2} + h^{2}}$.
  - Sphere: $V = \frac{4}{3} \pi r^{3}$, SA $= 4\pi r^{2}$.
- **Coordinate Geometry:**
  - Distance: $d = \sqrt{(x_{2}-x_{1})^{2} + (y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}}$.
  - Midpoint: $(\frac{x_{1}+x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1}+y_{2}}{2})$.
  - Slope: $m = \frac{y_{2}-y_{1}}{x_{2}-x_{1}}$.
  - Line: $y - y_{1} = m(x - x_{1})$.
  - Area of Triangle: $\frac{1}{2} |x_{1}(y_{2}-y_{3}) + x_{2}(y_{3}-y_{1}) + x_{3}(y_{1}-y_{2})|$.
:::

## Triangles & angles

**Q1.** In a triangle $ABC$, the angles are in the ratio $2:3:4$. What is the measure of the largest angle?
(a) $40^{\circ}$ (b) $60^{\circ}$ (c) $80^{\circ}$ (d) $100^{\circ}$

**Solution:**

The sum of angles in a triangle is $180^{\circ}$.

Let the angles be $2x$, $3x$, and $4x$.

$2x + 3x + 4x = 9x = 180^{\circ} \implies x = 20^{\circ}$.

The largest angle is $4x = 4 \times 20^{\circ} = 80^{\circ}$.

**Answer:** $80^{\circ}$ (c)

**Q2.** The sides of a triangle are $5$ cm, $12$ cm, and $13$ cm. What is the length of the altitude drawn to the longest side?
(a) $4.6$ cm (b) $5.2$ cm (c) $4.8$ cm (d) $6.0$ cm

**Solution:**

Since $5^{2} + 12^{2} = 25 + 144 = 169 = 13^{2}$, this is a right-angled triangle.

Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 12 = 30$ sq cm.

Alternatively, viewing the hypotenuse as the base: Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 13 \times h$.

$\frac{1}{2} \times 13 \times h = 30 \implies 13h = 60 \implies h = \frac{60}{13} \approx 4.61$ cm.

**Answer:** $4.6$ cm (a)

**Q3.** Two similar triangles have areas of $16$ sq cm and $49$ sq cm. If the median of the smaller triangle is $6$ cm, what is the corresponding median of the larger triangle?
(a) $8.5$ cm (b) $10.5$ cm (c) $12.5$ cm (d) $14.5$ cm

**Solution:**

For similar triangles, the ratio of their areas is the square of the ratio of their corresponding linear dimensions (sides, medians, altitudes).

$\frac{16}{49} = \left(\frac{6}{m_{2}}\right)^{2}$.

Taking the square root: $\frac{4}{7} = \frac{6}{m_{2}} \implies 4m_{2} = 42 \implies m_{2} = 10.5$ cm.

::: keypoint
Ratio of areas of similar figures = $(\text{Ratio of linear dimensions})^{2}$. Ratio of volumes = $(\text{Ratio of linear dimensions})^{3}$.
:::

**Answer:** $10.5$ cm (b)

## Circles & polygons

**Q4.** A regular polygon has an interior angle of $144^{\circ}$. How many sides does the polygon have?
(a) $8$ (b) $10$ (c) $12$ (d) $14$

**Solution:**

The sum of interior and exterior angles at any vertex is $180^{\circ}$.

Exterior angle $= 180^{\circ} - 144^{\circ} = 36^{\circ}$.

For any regular polygon, the sum of exterior angles is $360^{\circ}$.

Number of sides $n = \frac{360^{\circ}}{36^{\circ}} = 10$.

**Answer:** $10$ (b)

**Q5.** A central angle of $45^{\circ}$ subtends an arc of length $11$ cm. What is the radius of the circle? (Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$)
(a) $7$ cm (b) $14$ cm (c) $21$ cm (d) $28$ cm

**Solution:**

Arc length $= \frac{\theta}{360^{\circ}} \times 2\pi r$.

$11 = \frac{45^{\circ}}{360^{\circ}} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r$.

$11 = \frac{1}{8} \times \frac{44}{7} \times r \implies 11 = \frac{11}{14} \times r \implies r = 14$ cm.

**Answer:** $14$ cm (b)

**Q6.** From a point $P$ outside a circle with center $O$, two tangents $PA$ and $PB$ are drawn. If $\angle APB = 60^{\circ}$ and $PA = 8$ cm, what is the length of the chord $AB$?
(a) $4$ cm (b) $4\sqrt{3}$ cm (c) $8$ cm (d) $8\sqrt{3}$ cm

**Solution:**

Tangents from an external point are equal in length, so $PA = PB = 8$ cm.

In $\triangle APB$, since $PA = PB$, the angles opposite to them are equal: $\angle PAB = \angle PBA$.

In $\triangle APB$, sum of angles is $180^{\circ}$, so $\angle PAB = \angle PBA = \frac{180^{\circ} - 60^{\circ}}{2} = 60^{\circ}$.

Thus, $\triangle APB$ is an equilateral triangle.

Therefore, the chord $AB = PA = 8$ cm.

**Answer:** $8$ cm (c)

## 2D mensuration

**Q7.** The lengths of the sides of a triangle are $7$ cm, $24$ cm, and $25$ cm. What is its area?
(a) $72$ sq cm (b) $84$ sq cm (c) $96$ sq cm (d) $108$ sq cm

**Solution:**

Check for a Pythagorean triple: $7^{2} + 24^{2} = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^{2}$.

The triangle is a right-angled triangle.

Area $= \frac{1}{2} \times 7 \times 24 = 84$ sq cm.

**Answer:** $84$ sq cm (b)

**Q8.** A rectangular park is $45$ m long and $30$ m wide. A path $2.5$ m wide is constructed outside the park. What is the area of the path?
(a) $200$ sq m (b) $300$ sq m (c) $400$ sq m (d) $450$ sq m

**Solution:**

Area of the original park $= 45 \times 30 = 1350$ sq m.

The path surrounds the park, adding $2 \times 2.5 = 5$ m to both length and width.

New length $= 45 + 5 = 50$ m. New width $= 30 + 5 = 35$ m.

Area with path $= 50 \times 35 = 1750$ sq m.

Area of path $= 1750 - 1350 = 400$ sq m.

::: trap
A common mistake is adding the path width only once to the dimensions. The path extends on both sides, so add $2 \times \text{width}$.
:::

**Answer:** $400$ sq m (c)

**Q9.** If the radius of a circle is increased by $20\%$, by what percentage does its area increase?
(a) $20\%$ (b) $40\%$ (c) $44\%$ (d) $48\%$

**Solution:**

Area is proportional to $r^{2}$.

If the new radius is $1.2r$, the new area is proportional to $(1.2r)^{2} = 1.44r^{2}$.

This is a $44\%$ increase.

::: keypoint
For successive percentage changes (or squaring a changing parameter), use the net change formula: $a + b + \frac{ab}{100}\,\%$. Here, $20 + 20 + \frac{20 \times 20}{100} = 44\%$.
:::

**Answer:** $44\%$ (c)

**Q10.** Four maximum-sized equal circles are cut from a square cardboard of side $14$ cm. What is the area of the remaining cardboard? (Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$)
(a) $36$ sq cm (b) $42$ sq cm (c) $54$ sq cm (d) $62$ sq cm

**Solution:**

Area of the square $= 14^{2} = 196$ sq cm.

The 4 equal circles fit perfectly, arranged in a $2 \times 2$ grid.

The diameter of each circle is half the square's side: $D = 7$ cm, so $r = 3.5$ cm $= \frac{7}{2}$ cm.

Area of one circle $= \pi r^{2} = \frac{22}{7} \times \frac{7}{2} \times \frac{7}{2} = \frac{77}{2} = 38.5$ sq cm.

Total area of 4 circles $= 4 \times 38.5 = 154$ sq cm.

Remaining area $= 196 - 154 = 42$ sq cm.

**Answer:** $42$ sq cm (b)

## 3D mensuration

**Q11.** A metallic sphere of radius $10.5$ cm is melted and recast into small cones, each of radius $3.5$ cm and height $3$ cm. How many such cones can be made?
(a) $105$ (b) $126$ (c) $147$ (d) $168$

**Solution:**

Volume is conserved when recasting.

Volume of sphere $= \frac{4}{3} \pi R^{3} = \frac{4}{3} \pi (10.5)^{3}$.

Volume of one cone $= \frac{1}{3} \pi r^{2} h = \frac{1}{3} \pi (3.5)^{2} \times 3$.

Number of cones $N = \frac{\frac{4}{3} \pi (10.5)^{3}}{\frac{1}{3} \pi (3.5)^{2} \times 3}$.

Cancel $\frac{\pi}{3}$: $N = \frac{4 \times (10.5)^{3}}{3 \times (3.5)^{2}} = \frac{4 \times 10.5 \times 10.5 \times 10.5}{3 \times 3.5 \times 3.5}$.

Notice that $\frac{10.5}{3.5} = 3$.

$N = \frac{4 \times 3 \times 3 \times 10.5}{3} = 4 \times 3 \times 10.5 = 12 \times 10.5 = 126$.

**Answer:** $126$ (b)

**Q12.** If each edge of a cube is increased by $50\%$, what is the percentage increase in its surface area?
(a) $50\%$ (b) $75\%$ (c) $100\%$ (d) $125\%$

**Solution:**

Surface area is proportional to the square of the edge length ($6a^{2}$).

New edge length is $1.5a$.

New surface area is proportional to $(1.5a)^{2} = 2.25a^{2}$.

This is an increase of $1.25$ times, or $125\%$.

Using the successive change formula: $50 + 50 + \frac{50 \times 50}{100} = 100 + 25 = 125\%$.

**Answer:** $125\%$ (d)

**Q13.** A solid cylinder of radius $5$ cm and height $12$ cm is melted to form a solid cone of the same base radius. What is the height of the cone?
(a) $12$ cm (b) $24$ cm (c) $36$ cm (d) $48$ cm

**Solution:**

Volume of cylinder $= \pi r^{2} h = \pi (5)^{2}(12)$.

Volume of cone $= \frac{1}{3} \pi r^{2} H = \frac{1}{3} \pi (5)^{2} H$.

Equating volumes: $\pi (5)^{2}(12) = \frac{1}{3} \pi (5)^{2} H \implies 12 = \frac{1}{3} H \implies H = 36$ cm.

::: keypoint
A cylinder has exactly $3$ times the volume of a cone with the same base and height. If base areas match, the cone must be $3$ times taller to hold the same volume.
:::

**Answer:** $36$ cm (c)

**Q14.** A large cube is painted red on all its faces, and then cut into $64$ identical smaller cubes. How many small cubes have exactly one face painted?
(a) $8$ (b) $16$ (c) $24$ (d) $32$

**Solution:**

The large cube is divided into $64 = 4^{3}$ small cubes, so it is a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ grid. Let $n = 4$.

Cubes with exactly one face painted are located strictly in the interior of the faces (not on edges).

On each face, the number of such cubes is $(n - 2) \times (n - 2) = (4 - 2) \times (4 - 2) = 2 \times 2 = 4$.

Since a cube has $6$ faces, total cubes with one face painted $= 6 \times 4 = 24$.

**Answer:** $24$ (c)

## Coordinate geometry

**Q15.** What is the distance between the points $A(2, -3)$ and $B(5, 1)$?
(a) $4$ (b) $5$ (c) $6$ (d) $7$

**Solution:**

$d = \sqrt{(x_{2} - x_{1})^{2} + (y_{2} - y_{1})^{2}}$.

$d = \sqrt{(5 - 2)^{2} + (1 - (-3))^{2}} = \sqrt{3^{2} + 4^{2}} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

**Answer:** $5$ (b)

**Q16.** Find the ratio in which the $y$-axis divides the line segment joining the points $(-3, 5)$ and $(4, -2)$.
(a) $2:3$ (b) $3:4$ (c) $4:3$ (d) $1:2$

**Solution:**

Let the $y$-axis divide the line in the ratio $k:1$. The coordinates of the intersection point are $(0, y)$.

Using the section formula for the $x$-coordinate: $x = \frac{m_{1}x_{2} + m_{2}x_{1}}{m_{1} + m_{2}}$.

$0 = \frac{k(4) + 1(-3)}{k + 1} \implies 4k - 3 = 0 \implies k = \frac{3}{4}$.

The ratio is $3:4$.

**Answer:** $3:4$ (b)

**Q17.** Are the points $P(1, 2)$, $Q(3, 4)$, and $R(7, 8)$ collinear?
(a) Yes (b) No (c) Cannot be determined (d) None

**Solution:**

Points are collinear if the slope between any two pairs is identical, or if the area of the triangle formed is $0$.

Slope of $PQ = \frac{4 - 2}{3 - 1} = \frac{2}{2} = 1$.

Slope of $QR = \frac{8 - 4}{7 - 3} = \frac{4}{4} = 1$.

Since the slopes are equal, the points lie on the same straight line.

**Answer:** Yes (a)

**Q18.** What is the equation of the line passing through $(2, 3)$ and perpendicular to the line $3x - 4y + 5 = 0$?
(a) $4x + 3y = 17$ (b) $4x - 3y = -1$ (c) $3x + 4y = 18$ (d) $4x + 3y = 7$

**Solution:**

The given line is $3x - 4y + 5 = 0 \implies 4y = 3x + 5 \implies y = \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{5}{4}$. Its slope is $m_{1} = \frac{3}{4}$.

A perpendicular line has slope $m_{2} = -\frac{1}{m_{1}} = -\frac{4}{3}$.

Using the point-slope form $y - y_{1} = m_{2}(x - x_{1})$ with point $(2, 3)$:

$y - 3 = -\frac{4}{3}(x - 2)$.

$3(y - 3) = -4(x - 2) \implies 3y - 9 = -4x + 8 \implies 4x + 3y = 17$.

**Answer:** $4x + 3y = 17$ (a)

## Wrapped OA variants

**Q19.** A hall is $15$ m long and $12$ m wide. The floor is to be paved with square tiles of side $30$ cm. What is the total cost of paving if each tile costs $12$ dollars?
(a) $24000$ dollars (b) $21000$ dollars (c) $18000$ dollars (d) $15000$ dollars

**Solution:**

Convert dimensions to identical units. Hall is $1500$ cm $\times$ $1200$ cm.

Area of floor $= 1500 \times 1200 = 1800000$ sq cm.

Area of one tile $= 30 \times 30 = 900$ sq cm.

Number of tiles required $= \frac{1800000}{900} = 2000$.

Total cost $= 2000 \times 12 = 24000$ dollars.

::: interview
In many standard aptitude platforms, the currency might be INR (Rs). For international OAs, amounts are often in dollars. Ensure units ($m$ to $cm$) are standardized before dividing areas.
:::

**Answer:** $24000$ (a)

**Q20.** Water flows into a cylindrical tank of diameter $14$ m at a speed of $2$ km/hr through a rectangular pipe of dimensions $0.5$ m by $0.2$ m. What is the rise in the water level of the tank in $3$ hours? (Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$)
(a) $1.5$ m (b) $2.8$ m (c) $3.9$ m (d) $4.2$ m

**Solution:**

The speed of water is $2$ km/hr $= 2000$ m/hr.

In $1$ hour, the volume of water flowing through the pipe $= \text{Area of cross-section} \times \text{Speed}$.

Volume in $1$ hr $= (0.5 \times 0.2) \times 2000 = 0.1 \times 2000 = 200$ cubic meters.

In $3$ hours, total volume supplied $= 3 \times 200 = 600$ cubic meters.

This volume falls into a cylindrical tank of radius $R = \frac{14}{2} = 7$ m.

Volume in tank $= \pi R^{2} h = \frac{22}{7} \times 7^{2} \times h = 154h$.

Equating volumes: $154h = 600 \implies h = \frac{600}{154} = \frac{300}{77} \approx 3.896$ m.

::: heavy
Flow-rate problems bridge mensuration and time-speed. Treat the "length" of the water column supplied per unit time as exactly equal to the speed of the fluid.
:::

**Answer:** $3.9$ m (c)

## Inscribed & Circumscribed Figures

**Q21.** A circle is inscribed in a square, and a second circle is circumscribed about the same square. What is
the ratio of the area of the circumscribed circle to the area of the inscribed circle?
(a) $\sqrt{2}:1$ (b) $2:1$ (c) $4:1$ (d) $\pi:2$

**Solution:** Let the square have side $a$. The **inscribed** circle touches the sides, so its diameter equals
the side: $r_{\text{in}} = a/2$. The **circumscribed** circle passes through the corners, so its diameter equals
the diagonal $a\sqrt{2}$: $r_{\text{out}} = a\sqrt{2}/2$. The area ratio is

$\left(\dfrac{r_{\text{out}}}{r_{\text{in}}}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{a\sqrt{2}/2}{a/2}\right)^2 = (\sqrt{2})^2 = 2$.

**Answer:** $2:1$ (b)

::: keypoint
For a square of side $a$: inscribed circle radius $= a/2$, circumscribed circle radius $= a/\sqrt{2}$. The corner
circle always has **twice** the area of the edge circle, independent of $a$.
:::
