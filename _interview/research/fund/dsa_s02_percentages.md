# Percentages

::: keypoint
**Percentage as a Fraction:** $x\% = \frac{x}{100}$.

**Fraction ↔ Percentage Speed Table:**

| Fraction | Percentage | Fraction | Percentage |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $1/2$ | $50\%$ | $1/7$ | $14.28\%$ |
| $1/3$ | $33.33\%$ | $1/8$ | $12.5\%$ |
| $1/4$ | $25\%$ | $1/9$ | $11.11\%$ |
| $1/5$ | $20\%$ | $1/10$ | $10\%$ |
| $1/6$ | $16.66\%$ | $1/11$ | $9.09\%$ |

**Successive Change:**
For successive percentage changes $a\%$ then $b\%$, the net change is $a + b + \frac{ab}{100}\,\%$.

**The Reversal Trick:**
If a value increases by $x\%$, to return to the original value, it must be decreased by $\frac{100x}{100+x}\,\%$. Or more generally, if a base increases by a fraction $\frac{1}{n}$, to reverse it you decrease by $\frac{1}{n+1}$.
:::

## Basics & Conversions

**Q1.** What is $12.5\%$ of $840$?

(a) $105$ (b) $115$ (c) $125$ (d) $100$

**Solution:**
Use the fractional equivalent. $12.5\% = \frac{1}{8}$.
$$\frac{1}{8} \times 840 = 105$$

**Answer:** $105$ (a)

**Q2.** If $30\%$ of a number is $12.6$, what is the number?

(a) $42$ (b) $40$ (c) $45$ (d) $48$

**Solution:**
Let the number be $x$.
$$\frac{30}{100} \times x = 12.6 \implies x = \frac{12.6 \times 100}{30} = \frac{1260}{30} = 42$$

**Answer:** $42$ (a)

**Q3.** What percentage of $2.5$ kg is $50$ g?

(a) $2\%$ (b) $5\%$ (c) $20\%$ (d) $50\%$

**Solution:**
Convert everything to grams. $2.5 \text{ kg} = 2500 \text{ g}$.
$$\frac{50}{2500} \times 100\% = \frac{50}{25} \% = 2\%$$

**Answer:** $2\%$ (a)

## Percentage Change

**Q4.** The price of a laptop is first increased by $20\%$, and then decreased by $20\%$. What is the net percentage change in the price?

(a) $0\%$ (b) $4\%$ decrease (c) $4\%$ increase (d) $2\%$ decrease

**Solution:**
Use the successive change formula: $a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$. Here $a = +20, b = -20$.
$$20 - 20 + \frac{(20)(-20)}{100} = -\frac{400}{100} = -4\%$$
A negative sign indicates a decrease.

::: trap
A common mistake is assuming an equal percentage increase and decrease cancels out to $0\%$. An increase of $x\%$ followed by a decrease of $x\%$ always results in a net **loss** of $\frac{x^2}{100}\%$.
:::

**Answer:** $4\%$ decrease (b)

**Q5.** A rectangle's length is increased by $10\%$ and its width is decreased by $10\%$. What is the percentage change in its area?

(a) $0\%$ (b) $1\%$ decrease (c) $1\%$ increase (d) $10\%$ decrease

**Solution:**
Area is length $\times$ width, so changes in length and width act as successive percentage changes on the area.
Net change $= +10 - 10 - \frac{10 \times 10}{100} = -1\%$.

**Answer:** $1\%$ decrease (b)

**Q6.** The population of a town increases by $5\%$ annually. If its present population is $44100$, what was its population $2$ years ago?

(a) $40000$ (b) $41000$ (c) $42000$ (d) $43000$

**Solution:**
Let the population $2$ years ago be $P$. An annual increase of $5\%$ means multiplying by $1.05$ (or $\frac{21}{20}$) each year.
$$P \times \frac{21}{20} \times \frac{21}{20} = 44100$$
$$P \times \frac{441}{400} = 44100 \implies P = 40000$$

**Answer:** $40000$ (a)

**Q7.** A number is increased by $20\%$, then increased by $25\%$. What single percentage increase is this equivalent to?

(a) $45\%$ (b) $50\%$ (c) $40\%$ (d) $60\%$

**Solution:**
Use $a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$ with $a=20$ and $b=25$.
$$20 + 25 + \frac{20 \times 25}{100} = 45 + \frac{500}{100} = 45 + 5 = 50\%$$

**Answer:** $50\%$ (b)

## Reverse / Base Problems

**Q8.** If A's salary is $25\%$ more than B's salary, then B's salary is how much percent less than A's?

(a) $20\%$ (b) $25\%$ (c) $33.33\%$ (d) $15\%$

**Solution:**
$A = B + 0.25B = 1.25B \implies B = \frac{A}{1.25} = 0.8A$.
B is $20\%$ less than A.

::: keypoint
**Fractional shortcut:** If $A$ is $\frac{1}{n}$ more than $B$, then $B$ is $\frac{1}{n+1}$ less than $A$. Here $25\% = \frac{1}{4}$ more. Therefore, $B$ is $\frac{1}{4+1} = \frac{1}{5} = 20\%$ less.
:::

**Answer:** $20\%$ (a)

**Q9.** The price of petrol goes up by $10\%$. By what percentage must a consumer reduce their consumption so that their expenditure on petrol remains unchanged?

(a) $9.09\%$ (b) $10\%$ (c) $11.11\%$ (d) $12.5\%$

**Solution:**
Expenditure $=$ Price $\times$ Consumption.
To keep Expenditure constant, if Price increases by $10\%$ (a fraction of $\frac{1}{10}$), Consumption must decrease by $\frac{1}{10+1} = \frac{1}{11}$.
$$\frac{1}{11} \approx 9.09\%$$

**Answer:** $9.09\%$ (a)

**Q10.** Due to a $20\%$ reduction in the price of sugar, a purchaser can buy $4$ kg more for $\$160$. What is the original price of sugar per kg?

(a) $\$10$ (b) $\$8$ (c) $\$12$ (d) $\$15$

**Solution:**
A $20\%$ reduction means the price is now $\frac{4}{5}$ of the original.
To spend the same amount ($\$160$), consumption must increase by $\frac{1}{4}$ (using the reversal trick: if price decreases by $\frac{1}{5}$, consumption increases by $\frac{1}{5-1} = \frac{1}{4}$).
We are told the increase in consumption is $4$ kg. So, $\frac{1}{4}$ of the original consumption is $4$ kg.
Original consumption $= 16$ kg.
Original price $= \frac{\$160}{16 \text{ kg}} = \$10/\text{kg}$.

**Answer:** $\$10$ (a)

## Word Problems

**Q11.** In an examination, a student scores $30\%$ marks and fails by $15$ marks. Another student scores $40\%$ marks and gets $35$ marks more than the minimum passing marks. What is the maximum marks for the exam?

(a) $400$ (b) $500$ (c) $600$ (d) $700$

**Solution:**
Let the maximum marks be $M$.
The passing mark can be expressed in two ways:
$$0.30M + 15 = 0.40M - 35$$
$$0.10M = 50 \implies M = 500$$

**Answer:** $500$ (b)

**Q12.** In an election between two candidates, the winner got $65\%$ of the total valid votes. If the total votes were $6000$ and $20\%$ of them were invalid, what was the number of valid votes the losing candidate got?

(a) $1500$ (b) $1680$ (c) $1800$ (d) $3120$

**Solution:**
Total valid votes $= 80\%$ of $6000 = 4800$.
The winner got $65\%$, so the loser got $100\% - 65\% = 35\%$ of the valid votes.
Loser's votes $= 35\%$ of $4800 = 0.35 \times 4800 = 1680$.

**Answer:** $1680$ (b)

**Q13.** A man spends $20\%$ of his income on rent, $30\%$ of the remaining on food, and saves the rest. If he saves $\$8400$, what is his total income?

(a) $\$12000$ (b) $\$15000$ (c) $\$18000$ (d) $\$20000$

**Solution:**
Let total income be $I$.
After $20\%$ on rent, he has $80\%$ remaining ($0.8I$).
He spends $30\%$ *of the remaining* on food, which means he saves $70\%$ of the remaining.
Savings $= 0.70 \times (0.80 \times I) = 0.56I$.
$$0.56I = 8400 \implies I = \frac{8400}{0.56} = 15000$$

::: trap
Do not simply add $20\% + 30\% = 50\%$. The $30\%$ is calculated on the **remaining** amount, not the original total.
:::

**Answer:** $\$15000$ (b)

**Q14.** Fresh fruit contains $68\%$ water and dry fruit contains $20\%$ water. How much dry fruit can be obtained from $100$ kg of fresh fruit?

(a) $32$ kg (b) $40$ kg (c) $52$ kg (d) $80$ kg

**Solution:**
The core trick is that the **weight of the solid pulp remains constant**.
In $100$ kg of fresh fruit ($68\%$ water), the pulp is $32\%$.
$$\text{Pulp} = 32\% \text{ of } 100 \text{ kg} = 32 \text{ kg}$$
Let the weight of the dry fruit be $D$. It contains $20\%$ water, so it is $80\%$ pulp.
$$80\% \text{ of } D = 32 \text{ kg}$$
$$0.8D = 32 \implies D = \frac{32}{0.8} = 40 \text{ kg}$$

**Answer:** $40$ kg (b)

**Q15.** The passing percentage in an exam is $40\%$. If a candidate gets $250$ marks and fails by $38$ marks, what are the maximum marks?

(a) $700$ (b) $720$ (c) $800$ (d) $850$

**Solution:**
Passing marks $= 250 + 38 = 288$.
This represents $40\%$ of the total marks $M$.
$$0.4M = 288 \implies M = \frac{288}{0.4} = 720$$

**Answer:** $720$ (b)

## Wrapped OA Variants

**Q16.** A software company processes a massive dataset. During the first pass, $20\%$ of the corrupt records are removed. During a second specialized pass, $15\%$ of the remaining corrupt records are successfully repaired. If there are still $1700$ corrupt records in the system, how many corrupt records were there initially?

(a) $2000$ (b) $2500$ (c) $3000$ (d) $3500$

**Solution:**
Let the initial number of corrupt records be $C$.
After removing $20\%$, $80\%$ remain: $0.8C$.
After repairing $15\%$ of those, $85\%$ of them remain un-repaired.
$$\text{Un-repaired} = 0.85 \times (0.80C) = 0.68C$$
$$0.68C = 1700 \implies C = \frac{1700}{0.68} = 2500$$

**Answer:** $2500$ (b)

**Q17.** A cloud storage provider charges based on utilization. A server was at $60\%$ capacity. A new client was boarded, increasing the utilized capacity by $25\%$ of its current load. Later, optimization scripts reduced the overall utilized capacity by $10\%$ of its new load. What percentage of the total server capacity is now utilized?

(a) $67.5\%$ (b) $72\%$ (c) $75\%$ (d) $62.5\%$

**Solution:**
Let total capacity $= 100$ units. Initial utilization $= 60$ units.
Increase by $25\%$ *of the current load*: The load goes from $60 \to 60 \times 1.25 = 75$ units.
Decrease by $10\%$ *of the new load*: The load goes from $75 \to 75 \times 0.90 = 67.5$ units.
Since total capacity is $100$, the utilization is exactly $67.5\%$.

::: interview
In system design or technical OAs, watch out for the phrasing "increased to" versus "increased by". Here it is "increased by 25% of its current load", meaning it acts as a successive percentage change on the $60\%$, not an additive $+25\%$ to the total server capacity.
:::

**Answer:** $67.5\%$ (a)

**Q18.** An e-commerce platform applies a dynamic discount on an item originally priced at $\$200$. First, a seasonal discount of $15\%$ is applied. Then, a loyalty member discount of $x\%$ is applied on the discounted price. If the final price paid by the loyalty member is $\$136$, what is the value of $x$?

(a) $10\%$ (b) $15\%$ (c) $20\%$ (d) $25\%$

**Solution:**
Price after first discount $= 200 \times (1 - 0.15) = 200 \times 0.85 = \$170$.
Let the second discount be $x\%$. The price becomes $170 \times (1 - \frac{x}{100}) = 136$.
$$1 - \frac{x}{100} = \frac{136}{170}$$
$$\frac{136}{170} = \frac{136 \div 34}{170 \div 34} = \frac{4}{5} = 0.8$$
So, $1 - \frac{x}{100} = 0.8 \implies \frac{x}{100} = 0.2 \implies x = 20$.
The loyalty discount is $20\%$.

**Answer:** $20$ (c)
