# Ages & Partnership

::: keypoint
**Ages:** Always set the *present* age as the variable (e.g., let the present age be $x$). Translate "$N$ years ago" to $x - N$ and "$N$ years hence/later" to $x + N$. Set up equations directly from the ratio conditions given in the problem.
**Partnership:** Profits are split in the ratio of $\text{Capital} \times \text{Time}$. If partner A invests $C_{A}$ for $T_{A}$ months and partner B invests $C_{B}$ for $T_{B}$ months, the profit ratio is $C_{A} T_{A} : C_{B} T_{B}$. 
:::

## Ages

**Q1.** The ratio of the present ages of a mother and her daughter is $7:1$. Four years ago, the ratio of their ages was $19:1$. What will be the mother's age 4 years from now?
(a) 42 years (b) 46 years (c) 50 years (d) 52 years

**Solution:** Let the present ages of the mother and daughter be $7x$ and $x$ respectively. Four years ago, their ages were $7x - 4$ and $x - 4$.
Set up the ratio equation: $\frac{7x - 4}{x - 4} = 19$. 
Solving for $x$: $7x - 4 = 19x - 76 \implies 12x = 72 \implies x = 6$.
The mother's present age is $7(6) = 42$. Her age 4 years from now will be $42 + 4 = 46$.

**Answer:** 46 years (b)

**Q2.** The sum of the ages of a father and his son is 45 years. Five years ago, the product of their ages was 34. What is the son's present age?
(a) 6 years (b) 7 years (c) 8 years (d) 9 years

**Solution:** Let the father's present age be $f$ and the son's be $s$, so $f + s = 45$.
Five years ago, their ages were $f - 5$ and $s - 5$. 
Substitute $f = 45 - s$ into the product condition: $(40 - s)(s - 5) = 34$.
Expand: $40s - 200 - s^{2} + 5s = 34 \implies s^{2} - 45s + 234 = 0$.
Factor the quadratic: $(s - 6)(s - 39) = 0$. Since the son is the younger one, $s = 6$.

**Answer:** 6 years (a)

**Q3.** A father is currently three times as old as his son. Eight years from now, he will be two and a half times as old as his son. How old is the father right now?
(a) 48 years (b) 60 years (c) 72 years (d) 84 years

**Solution:** Let the son's present age be $x$, making the father's age $3x$.
In 8 years, they will be $x + 8$ and $3x + 8$.
Equation: $3x + 8 = 2.5(x + 8)$.
Multiply by 2 to clear decimals: $6x + 16 = 5(x + 8) \implies 6x + 16 = 5x + 40 \implies x = 24$.
The father's present age is $3 \times 24 = 72$.

**Answer:** 72 years (c)

**Q4.** Person A is 24 years older than person B. Ten years ago, A was five times as old as B. What is B's present age?
(a) 14 years (b) 16 years (c) 18 years (d) 20 years

**Solution:** The age difference between two people is constant at any point in time. 
Ten years ago, the difference was still 24. 
Let B's age 10 years ago be $y$. Then A's age 10 years ago was $5y$.
Difference: $5y - y = 24 \implies 4y = 24 \implies y = 6$.
B's age 10 years ago was 6, making B's present age $6 + 10 = 16$.

**Answer:** 16 years (b)

::: trap
The most common mistake in ages problems is applying a sum, product, or difference constraint to a ratio from a *different* point in time. If a ratio describes the situation 10 years ago, you must offset all present ages by $-10$ before calculating.
:::

**Q5.** The sum of the present ages of X and Y is 60 years. Six years ago, X's age was exactly three times Y's age. What is X's present age?
(a) 42 years (b) 45 years (c) 48 years (d) 50 years

**Solution:** If the sum of their present ages is 60, the sum of their ages 6 years ago was $60 - 2(6) = 48$.
Six years ago, they were in a $3:1$ ratio, meaning X was $\frac{3}{4}$ of the total.
X's age 6 years ago = $48 \times \frac{3}{4} = 36$.
X's present age = $36 + 6 = 42$.

**Answer:** 42 years (a)

**Q6.** Ten years ago, P was half of Q in age. If the ratio of their present ages is $3:4$, what is the total sum of their present ages?
(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 35 years (d) 40 years

**Solution:** Let their present ages be $3x$ and $4x$. 
Ten years ago, they were $3x - 10$ and $4x - 10$.
Condition: $3x - 10 = \frac{1}{2}(4x - 10)$.
Multiply by 2: $6x - 20 = 4x - 10 \implies 2x = 10 \implies x = 5$.
Their present ages are $15$ and $20$. Sum = $15 + 20 = 35$.

**Answer:** 35 years (c)

## Partnership

**Q7.** Alice and Bob start a business investing \$45,000 and \$60,000 respectively. If the total profit at the end of the year is \$35,000, what is Bob's share?
(a) \$15,000 (b) \$17,500 (c) \$20,000 (d) \$25,000

**Solution:** Both invested for the same duration (1 year), so the profit splits strictly by capital ratio.
Ratio = $45,000 : 60,000 = 3:4$.
Bob gets $\frac{4}{7}$ of the total profit.
Bob's share = $35,000 \times \frac{4}{7} = 20,000$.

**Answer:** \$20,000 (c)

**Q8.** X started a business with \$21,000. After 4 months, Y joined with \$36,000. At the end of the year, they made a total profit of \$30,000. What is X's share of the profit?
(a) \$12,000 (b) \$14,000 (c) \$16,000 (d) \$18,000

**Solution:** Calculate the weight ($\text{Capital} \times \text{Time}$) for each partner.
X invested for 12 months: $21 \times 12$.
Y joined after 4 months, so Y invested for 8 months: $36 \times 8$.
Profit Ratio (X:Y) = $(21 \times 12) : (36 \times 8) = 252 : 288$.
Simplify by dividing by 36: $7 : 8$. Total parts = 15.
X's share = $30,000 \times \frac{7}{15} = 14,000$.

**Answer:** \$14,000 (b)

**Q9.** A, B, and C enter into a partnership for a year. A invests 3 times as much as B, and B invests two-thirds of what C invests. At the end of the year, the profit is \$6,600. What is B's share?
(a) \$1,000 (b) \$1,200 (c) \$1,500 (d) \$1,800

**Solution:** Time is constant, so just find the unified capital ratio.
Let C's investment be $3x$ (choosing a multiple of 3 to avoid fractions).
Then B's investment = $\frac{2}{3}(3x) = 2x$.
A's investment = $3(2x) = 6x$.
Ratio A:B:C = $6 : 2 : 3$. Total parts = 11.
B's share = $6,600 \times \frac{2}{11} = 1,200$.

**Answer:** \$1,200 (b)

**Q10.** P invests \$4,000 for 8 months and \$6,000 for the remaining 4 months of the year. Q invests \$5,000 for the entire year. Out of a total profit of \$14,500, what is P's share?
(a) \$6,500 (b) \$7,000 (c) \$7,500 (d) \$8,000

**Solution:** P's capital changes mid-year, so break it into phases and sum the weights.
P's effective weight = $(4,000 \times 8) + (6,000 \times 4) = 32,000 + 24,000 = 56,000$.
Q's effective weight = $5,000 \times 12 = 60,000$.
Ratio P:Q = $56 : 60 = 14 : 15$. Total parts = 29.
P's share = $14,500 \times \frac{14}{29} = 7,000$.

**Answer:** \$7,000 (b)

**Q11.** A and B run a joint venture. A is a working partner and takes 20% of the total profit as his salary. The remaining profit is divided according to their capitals. A invested \$40,000 and B invested \$60,000. If A receives a total of \$26,000, what was the total profit?
(a) \$45,000 (b) \$50,000 (c) \$55,000 (d) \$60,000

**Solution:** Let the total profit be $P$.
A takes $0.2P$ off the top. The remainder to be split is $0.8P$.
Capital ratio A:B = $40 : 60 = 2 : 3$.
A's share of the remainder = $0.8P \times \frac{2}{5} = 0.32P$.
A's total receipt = Salary + Split = $0.2P + 0.32P = 0.52P$.
Set equation: $0.52P = 26,000 \implies P = \frac{26,000}{0.52} = 50,000$.

**Answer:** \$50,000 (b)

**Q12.** A invests $\frac{1}{6}$ of the total capital for $\frac{1}{6}$ of the total time. B invests $\frac{1}{3}$ of the capital for $\frac{1}{3}$ of the time. C invests the remaining capital for the whole time. If the total profit is \$46,000, what is B's share?
(a) \$4,000 (b) \$6,000 (c) \$8,000 (d) \$10,000

**Solution:** 
C's capital fraction = $1 - (\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3}) = 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}$.
Calculate weights ($\text{fraction of capital} \times \text{fraction of time}$):
A's weight = $\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{36}$.
B's weight = $\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{9} = \frac{4}{36}$.
C's weight = $\frac{1}{2} \times 1 = \frac{1}{2} = \frac{18}{36}$.
Ratio A:B:C = $1 : 4 : 18$. Total parts = 23.
B's share = $46,000 \times \frac{4}{23} = 8,000$.

**Answer:** \$8,000 (c)

## Wrapped OA Variants

::: interview
In modern OAs, basic partnership math is frequently disguised as startup equity vesting or shared-resource billing (e.g., AWS server costs split across teams). The underlying math—weighting resources by usage duration—is identical.
:::

**Q13.** Three developers, DevX, DevY, and DevZ, launch a SaaS startup. DevX puts in \$12,000 and works from month 1. DevY joins 3 months later, investing \$16,000. DevZ joins 2 months after DevY, investing \$27,000, but only stays for 4 months before leaving the venture. At the end of the first year (12 months), the startup pays out an \$88,000 founders' dividend based on capital-time contribution. How much does DevY receive?
(a) \$24,000 (b) \$32,000 (c) \$36,000 (d) \$40,000

**Solution:** Treat this as a standard partnership with different timeframes.
DevX is active for 12 months. Weight = $12,000 \times 12 = 144,000$.
DevY joins 3 months late, so is active for 9 months. Weight = $16,000 \times 9 = 144,000$.
DevZ joins at month 5 (3+2), but explicitly only stays for 4 months. Weight = $27,000 \times 4 = 108,000$.
Ratio = $144 : 144 : 108$. Divide by 12 to get $12 : 12 : 9$, and by 3 to get $4 : 4 : 3$.
Total parts = $11$.
DevY's share = $\$88,000 \times \frac{4}{11} = 32,000$.

**Answer:** \$32,000 (b)

**Q14.** A cloud engineering team rents a high-compute cluster for \$8,800 per month. They split the cost among three projects (P1, P2, and P3) proportionally based on node-usage time. P1 uses 40 nodes for 6 hours a day. P2 uses 60 nodes for 4 hours a day. P3 uses 80 nodes for 5 hours a day. How much of the monthly bill should be charged to P1?
(a) \$1,800 (b) \$2,000 (c) \$2,200 (d) \$2,400

**Solution:** The "capital" is the number of nodes, and "time" is the hours of usage. 
P1 weight = $40 \times 6 = 240$.
P2 weight = $60 \times 4 = 240$.
P3 weight = $80 \times 5 = 400$.
Ratio P1:P2:P3 = $240 : 240 : 400 = 3 : 3 : 5$. Total parts = 11.
P1's charge = $\$8,800 \times \frac{3}{11} = 2,400$.

**Answer:** \$2,400 (d)
