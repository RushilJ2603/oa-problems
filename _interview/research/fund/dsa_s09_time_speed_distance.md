# Time, Speed & Distance; Trains, Boats & Races

::: definition
**Time, Speed & Distance:**

- $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$
- **Unit conversion:** $1\text{ km/h} = \frac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}$. To convert m/s to km/h, multiply by $\frac{18}{5}$.
- **Average speed:** $\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}$. For two equal distances covered at speeds $a$ and $b$, Average Speed $= \frac{2ab}{a+b}$.
- **Relative speed:**
  - Opposite directions: $S_1 + S_2$
  - Same direction: $|S_1 - S_2|$
:::

## Basics & Unit Conversion

**Q1.** A person crosses a $600\text{ m}$ long street in $5\text{ minutes}$. What is his speed in km per hour?
(a) $3.6$ (b) $7.2$ (c) $8.4$ (d) $10$

**Solution:**
Time $= 5\text{ mins} = 300\text{ s}$. Speed $= \frac{600}{300} = 2\text{ m/s}$.
Convert to km/h: $2 \times \frac{18}{5} = 7.2\text{ km/h}$.

**Answer:** $7.2$ (b)

**Q2.** An aeroplane covers a certain distance at a speed of $240\text{ km/h}$ in $5\text{ hours}$. To cover the same distance in $1\frac{2}{3}\text{ hours}$, it must travel at a speed of:
(a) $300\text{ km/h}$ (b) $360\text{ km/h}$ (c) $600\text{ km/h}$ (d) $720\text{ km/h}$

**Solution:**
Distance $= 240 \times 5 = 1200\text{ km}$.
New time $= 1\frac{2}{3} = \frac{5}{3}\text{ hours}$.
Required speed $= \frac{1200}{5/3} = 1200 \times \frac{3}{5} = 720\text{ km/h}$.

**Answer:** $720\text{ km/h}$ (d)

**Q3.** Walking at $\frac{3}{4}$ of his usual speed, a man is $1.5\text{ hours}$ late. His usual time to cover the same distance is:
(a) $4.5\text{ hours}$ (b) $5.5\text{ hours}$ (c) $6\text{ hours}$ (d) $7.5\text{ hours}$

**Solution:**
Distance is constant, so speed and time are inversely proportional.
If new speed is $\frac{3}{4}S$, new time is $\frac{4}{3}T$.
Difference in time $= \frac{4}{3}T - T = \frac{1}{3}T = 1.5\text{ hours}$.
Usual time $T = 1.5 \times 3 = 4.5\text{ hours}$.

::: keypoint
If new speed is $\frac{a}{b}$ of usual speed, new time is $\frac{b}{a}$ of usual time. The delay is $(\frac{b}{a} - 1)T$.
:::

**Answer:** $4.5\text{ hours}$ (a)

## Average Speed

**Q4.** A man travels from point A to B at $40\text{ km/h}$ and returns from B to A at $60\text{ km/h}$. What is his average speed for the whole journey?
(a) $48\text{ km/h}$ (b) $50\text{ km/h}$ (c) $52\text{ km/h}$ (d) $54\text{ km/h}$

**Solution:**
The distances are equal. Do not just average the speeds!
Average speed $= \frac{2ab}{a+b} = \frac{2 \times 40 \times 60}{40 + 60} = \frac{4800}{100} = 48\text{ km/h}$.

::: trap
Averaging the speeds $\frac{40+60}{2}=50$ is a classic error. You spend *more time* travelling at the slower speed, so the true average is weighted toward the slower speed.
:::

**Answer:** $48\text{ km/h}$ (a)

**Q5.** A car travels the first one-third of a certain distance at $10\text{ km/h}$, the next one-third at $20\text{ km/h}$, and the last one-third at $60\text{ km/h}$. The average speed of the car for the whole journey is:
(a) $18\text{ km/h}$ (b) $24\text{ km/h}$ (c) $30\text{ km/h}$ (d) $36\text{ km/h}$

**Solution:**
Let total distance be $d = 60\text{ km}$ (LCM of 10, 20, 60 to avoid fractions).
Each third is $20\text{ km}$.
Time for leg 1: $\frac{20}{10} = 2\text{ h}$.
Time for leg 2: $\frac{20}{20} = 1\text{ h}$.
Time for leg 3: $\frac{20}{60} = \frac{1}{3}\text{ h}$.
Total time $= 2 + 1 + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{10}{3}\text{ h}$.
Average speed $= \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}} = \frac{60}{10/3} = 18\text{ km/h}$.

**Answer:** $18\text{ km/h}$ (a)

**Q6.** A person travels equal distances with speeds of $3\text{ km/h}$, $4\text{ km/h}$, and $5\text{ km/h}$. He takes a total of $47\text{ minutes}$. Find the total distance.
(a) $2\text{ km}$ (b) $3\text{ km}$ (c) $4\text{ km}$ (d) $5\text{ km}$

**Solution:**
Let each leg be $d\text{ km}$. Total distance $= 3d$.
Time $= \frac{d}{3} + \frac{d}{4} + \frac{d}{5} = \frac{20d + 15d + 12d}{60} = \frac{47d}{60}\text{ hours}$.
Given total time is $47\text{ minutes} = \frac{47}{60}\text{ hours}$.
So, $\frac{47d}{60} = \frac{47}{60} \implies d = 1\text{ km}$.
Total distance $= 3d = 3\text{ km}$.

**Answer:** $3\text{ km}$ (b)

## Relative Speed

**Q7.** Two men start together to walk a certain distance, one at $4\text{ km/h}$ and another at $3\text{ km/h}$. The former arrives half an hour before the latter. Find the distance.
(a) $6\text{ km}$ (b) $8\text{ km}$ (c) $9\text{ km}$ (d) $12\text{ km}$

**Solution:**
Let distance be $d$. Difference in time $= \frac{d}{3} - \frac{d}{4} = \frac{1}{2}\text{ hour}$.
$\frac{d}{12} = \frac{1}{2} \implies d = 6\text{ km}$.

**Answer:** $6\text{ km}$ (a)

**Q8.** A thief is spotted by a policeman from a distance of $200\text{ metres}$. When the policeman starts the chase, the thief also starts running. If the speed of the thief be $10\text{ km/h}$ and that of the policeman $12\text{ km/h}$, how far will the thief have run before he is overtaken?
(a) $1\text{ km}$ (b) $1.5\text{ km}$ (c) $2\text{ km}$ (d) $2.4\text{ km}$

**Solution:**
Relative speed $= 12 - 10 = 2\text{ km/h}$.
Time to close $200\text{ m}$ ($0.2\text{ km}$) gap $= \frac{0.2}{2} = 0.1\text{ hours}$.
Distance run by thief $= 10\text{ km/h} \times 0.1\text{ hours} = 1\text{ km}$.

**Answer:** $1\text{ km}$ (a)

**Q9.** Two stations A and B are $110\text{ km}$ apart on a straight line. One train starts from A at 7 a.m. and travels towards B at $20\text{ km/h}$. Another train starts from B at 8 a.m. and travels towards A at $25\text{ km/h}$. At what time will they meet?
(a) 9 a.m. (b) 10 a.m. (c) 11 a.m. (d) 12 noon

**Solution:**
First train travels for $1\text{ hour}$ alone from 7 a.m. to 8 a.m. Distance covered $= 20\text{ km}$.
Remaining distance at 8 a.m. $= 110 - 20 = 90\text{ km}$.
Relative speed (opposite directions) $= 20 + 25 = 45\text{ km/h}$.
Time to meet $= \frac{90}{45} = 2\text{ hours}$ after 8 a.m.
They meet at 10 a.m.

**Answer:** 10 a.m. (b)

## Trains

**Q10.** A train $150\text{ m}$ long is running with a speed of $68\text{ km/h}$. In what time will it pass a man who is running at $8\text{ km/h}$ in the same direction in which the train is going?
(a) $8\text{ sec}$ (b) $8.5\text{ sec}$ (c) $9\text{ sec}$ (d) $9.5\text{ sec}$

**Solution:**
Relative speed $= 68 - 8 = 60\text{ km/h}$.
Convert to m/s: $60 \times \frac{5}{18} = \frac{50}{3}\text{ m/s}$.
Distance to cover $= \text{length of train} = 150\text{ m}$.
Time $= \frac{150}{50/3} = 150 \times \frac{3}{50} = 9\text{ seconds}$.

**Answer:** $9\text{ sec}$ (c)

**Q11.** A train $240\text{ m}$ long passes a pole in $24\text{ seconds}$. How long will it take to pass a platform $650\text{ m}$ long?
(a) $65\text{ sec}$ (b) $89\text{ sec}$ (c) $100\text{ sec}$ (d) $150\text{ sec}$

**Solution:**
Speed of train $= \frac{240}{24} = 10\text{ m/s}$.
To pass the platform, total distance $= \text{length of train} + \text{length of platform} = 240 + 650 = 890\text{ m}$.
Time $= \frac{890}{10} = 89\text{ seconds}$.

**Answer:** $89\text{ sec}$ (b)

**Q12.** Two trains of equal length are running on parallel lines in the same direction at $46\text{ km/h}$ and $36\text{ km/h}$. The faster train passes the slower train in $36\text{ seconds}$. The length of each train is:
(a) $50\text{ m}$ (b) $72\text{ m}$ (c) $80\text{ m}$ (d) $82\text{ m}$

**Solution:**
Relative speed $= 46 - 36 = 10\text{ km/h} = 10 \times \frac{5}{18} = \frac{25}{9}\text{ m/s}$.
Distance covered to pass $= 2L$ (sum of both lengths).
Distance $= \text{speed} \times \text{time} = \frac{25}{9} \times 36 = 100\text{ m}$.
$2L = 100 \implies L = 50\text{ m}$.

**Answer:** $50\text{ m}$ (a)

**Q13.** Two trains running in opposite directions cross a man standing on the platform in $27\text{ seconds}$ and $17\text{ seconds}$ respectively, and they cross each other in $23\text{ seconds}$. The ratio of their speeds is:
(a) $1:3$ (b) $3:2$ (c) $3:4$ (d) $None$

**Solution:**
Let the speeds be $S_1$ and $S_2$.
Lengths of trains are $L_1 = 27S_1$ and $L_2 = 17S_2$.
Time to cross each other $= \frac{L_1 + L_2}{S_1 + S_2} = 23$.
$\frac{27S_1 + 17S_2}{S_1 + S_2} = 23 \implies 27S_1 + 17S_2 = 23S_1 + 23S_2 \implies 4S_1 = 6S_2 \implies \frac{S_1}{S_2} = \frac{6}{4} = \frac{3}{2}$.

::: keypoint
You can use alligation for this! Times to cross a point are $27$ and $17$. Time to cross each other is $23$ (mixture). The ratio of speeds is inversely proportional to the differences: $(23-17) : (27-23) = 6 : 4 = 3 : 2$.
:::

**Answer:** $3:2$ (b)

## Boats & Streams

::: definition
**Boats & Streams:**
Let $u$ be the speed of the boat in still water and $v$ be the speed of the stream.

- Speed downstream (with the current): $D = u + v$
- Speed upstream (against the current): $U = u - v$
- $u = \frac{D + U}{2}$ and $v = \frac{D - U}{2}$
:::

**Q14.** A boat can travel with a speed of $13\text{ km/h}$ in still water. If the speed of the stream is $4\text{ km/h}$, find the time taken by the boat to go $68\text{ km}$ downstream.
(a) $2\text{ hours}$ (b) $3\text{ hours}$ (c) $4\text{ hours}$ (d) $5\text{ hours}$

**Solution:**
Downstream speed $D = u + v = 13 + 4 = 17\text{ km/h}$.
Time $= \frac{\text{Distance}}{D} = \frac{68}{17} = 4\text{ hours}$.

**Answer:** $4\text{ hours}$ (c)

**Q15.** A man can row upstream at $7\text{ km/h}$ and downstream at $10\text{ km/h}$. Find man's rate in still water and the rate of current.
(a) $8.5, 1.5$ (b) $9, 1$ (c) $8, 2$ (d) $7.5, 2.5$

**Solution:**
$U = 7$, $D = 10$.
Speed in still water $u = \frac{10 + 7}{2} = 8.5\text{ km/h}$.
Rate of current $v = \frac{10 - 7}{2} = 1.5\text{ km/h}$.

**Answer:** $8.5\text{ km/h}, 1.5\text{ km/h}$ (a)

**Q16.** A motorboat, whose speed in still water is $15\text{ km/h}$, goes $30\text{ km}$ downstream and comes back in a total of $4\text{ hours } 30\text{ minutes}$. The speed of the stream is:
(a) $4\text{ km/h}$ (b) $5\text{ km/h}$ (c) $6\text{ km/h}$ (d) $10\text{ km/h}$

**Solution:**
Let stream speed be $v$. Downstream speed $= 15 + v$, Upstream speed $= 15 - v$.
$\frac{30}{15+v} + \frac{30}{15-v} = 4.5$
$30 \left( \frac{15-v+15+v}{225 - v^2} \right) = 4.5 \implies \frac{900}{225 - v^2} = 4.5$
$900 = 4.5(225 - v^2) \implies 200 = 225 - v^2 \implies v^2 = 25 \implies v = 5\text{ km/h}$.

**Answer:** $5\text{ km/h}$ (b)

**Q17.** A man rows to a place $48\text{ km}$ distant and comes back in $14\text{ hours}$. He finds that he can row $4\text{ km}$ with the stream in the same time as $3\text{ km}$ against the stream. The rate of the stream is:
(a) $1\text{ km/h}$ (b) $1.5\text{ km/h}$ (c) $2\text{ km/h}$ (d) $2.5\text{ km/h}$

**Solution:**
Since time is constant, speed is proportional to distance.
$\frac{D}{U} = \frac{4}{3} \implies D = 4x$ and $U = 3x$.
Total time $= \frac{48}{D} + \frac{48}{U} = 14 \implies \frac{48}{4x} + \frac{48}{3x} = 14 \implies \frac{12}{x} + \frac{16}{x} = 14 \implies \frac{28}{x} = 14 \implies x = 2$.
So $D = 8\text{ km/h}$ and $U = 6\text{ km/h}$.
Rate of stream $v = \frac{D - U}{2} = \frac{8 - 6}{2} = 1\text{ km/h}$.

**Answer:** $1\text{ km/h}$ (a)

## Races & Circular Tracks

::: keypoint
**Circular Track Rule (Meeting points):**
If two runners start from the same point at speeds $A$ and $B$, running in the *same* direction, the number of distinct meeting points on the track is $|A - B|$ (if $A,B$ are coprime integers).
In opposite directions, they meet at $A + B$ distinct points.
If you need the time for the $k$-th meeting on a circular track (length $L$, relative speed $v_{\text{rel}}$), the $k$-th meeting happens when total relative distance covered is $k \times L$.
:::

**Q18.** In a $100\text{ m}$ race, A covers the distance in $36\text{ seconds}$ and B in $45\text{ seconds}$. In this race, A beats B by:
(a) $20\text{ m}$ (b) $25\text{ m}$ (c) $22.5\text{ m}$ (d) $9\text{ m}$

**Solution:**
A finishes in $36\text{ s}$.
Where is B at $36\text{ s}$? B's speed is $\frac{100}{45} = \frac{20}{9}\text{ m/s}$.
Distance B covers in $36\text{ s} = \frac{20}{9} \times 36 = 80\text{ m}$.
Therefore, A beats B by $100 - 80 = 20\text{ m}$.

**Answer:** $20\text{ m}$ (a)

**Q19.** In a $1\text{ km}$ race, A beats B by $28\text{ m}$ or $7\text{ seconds}$. Find A's time over the course.
(a) $3$ minutes $47$ seconds
(b) $4$ minutes $3$ seconds
(c) $4$ minutes $10$ seconds
(d) $4$ minutes $17$ seconds

**Solution:**
B falls behind by $28\text{ m}$, and this takes B $7\text{ seconds}$ to cover.
So B's speed $= \frac{28}{7} = 4\text{ m/s}$.
B's time for $1000\text{ m} = \frac{1000}{4} = 250\text{ seconds}$.
A beats B by $7\text{ seconds}$, so A's time $= 250 - 7 = 243\text{ seconds}$.
$243\text{ seconds} = 4\text{ minutes and } 3\text{ seconds}$.

**Answer:** $4$ minutes $3$ seconds (b)

**Q20.** Two runners are running in opposite directions on a circular track of length $400\text{ m}$. Their speeds are $5\text{ m/s}$ and $3\text{ m/s}$. They start at the same point. When will they meet for the 3rd time?
(a) $150\text{ s}$ (b) $120\text{ s}$ (c) $50\text{ s}$ (d) $200\text{ s}$

**Solution:**
Relative speed (opposite directions) $= 5 + 3 = 8\text{ m/s}$.
To meet for the 1st time, they must cover a combined distance equal to $1$ track length $= 400\text{ m}$.
Time for 1st meeting $= \frac{400}{8} = 50\text{ seconds}$.
Each subsequent meeting takes another $50\text{ seconds}$.
Time for 3rd meeting $= 3 \times 50 = 150\text{ seconds}$.

**Answer:** $150\text{ s}$ (a)

## Wrapped OA Variants

::: interview
In actual assessment centers (like Cisco, Microsoft, or Optiver), standard relative-speed formulas are often hidden behind "patrol agents", "clock hands", or "data packets" to test your ability to map the text to the math.
:::

**Q21.** Two security robots patrol a bounded $5\text{ km}$ straight corridor. Robot A starts at the west end moving east at $3\text{ m/s}$. Robot B starts at the east end moving west at $2\text{ m/s}$. When either robot reaches a wall, it instantly reverses direction. After how many seconds do they cross paths for the second time?
(a) $1000\text{ s}$ (b) $2000\text{ s}$ (c) $3000\text{ s}$ (d) $5000\text{ s}$

**Solution:**
The corridor is $D = 5000\text{ m}$.
For their 1st meeting, the sum of their distances is $D$.
For their 2nd meeting, the total distance covered jointly by *both* robots between the two walls is exactly $3D$. (They each reach a wall and turn back).
Total distance jointly covered for 2nd meeting $= 3 \times 5000 = 15000\text{ m}$.
Relative speed (they are always moving towards each other relative to total joint distance) $= 3 + 2 = 5\text{ m/s}$.
Time $= \frac{15000}{5} = 3000\text{ seconds}$.

**Answer:** $3000\text{ s}$ (c)

**Q22.** A freight train leaves a warehouse at 6:00 AM heading north at $40\text{ km/h}$. At 8:00 AM, a fast passenger train leaves the same warehouse on a parallel track heading north at $90\text{ km/h}$. A drone is launched from the passenger train exactly at 8:00 AM, flies to the freight train at $120\text{ km/h}$, touches it, and instantly flies back to the passenger train, repeating this back-and-forth until the passenger train catches up to the freight train. What is the total distance flown by the drone?
(a) $120\text{ km}$ (b) $192\text{ km}$ (c) $200\text{ km}$ (d) $240\text{ km}$

**Solution:**
Don't sum an infinite geometric series of drone flights! Find the *total time* the drone is flying.
At 8:00 AM, the freight train has a head start of $2\text{ hours} \times 40\text{ km/h} = 80\text{ km}$.
Relative speed of passenger train closing the gap $= 90 - 40 = 50\text{ km/h}$.
Time to catch up $= \frac{80\text{ km}}{50\text{ km/h}} = 1.6\text{ hours}$.
The drone flies continuously for this entire $1.6\text{ hours}$ at $120\text{ km/h}$.
Total distance flown by drone $= 120 \times 1.6 = 192\text{ km}$.

::: keypoint
This is the classic "Von Neumann's Fly" puzzle, repackaged. Ignore the zig-zags. `Total Distance = Drone Speed × Time to intercept`.
:::

**Answer:** $192\text{ km}$ (b)

## Escalators & the Two-Trains Classic

**Q23.** A boy walks up a moving-up escalator and counts $30$ steps to reach the top. His sister walks up the same
escalator at twice his stepping rate and counts $40$ steps. How many steps are visible when the escalator is
switched off?
(a) $50$ (b) $60$ (c) $72$ (d) $80$

**Solution:** Let the boy step at rate $b$ and the escalator move at rate $e$ (steps per unit time). The boy's
$30$ steps take time $30/b$, during which the escalator adds $30e/b$ steps, so the total visible is
$N = 30 + \dfrac{30e}{b}$. The sister steps at $2b$: her $40$ steps take $40/(2b) = 20/b$, giving
$N = 40 + \dfrac{20e}{b}$. Equating: $30 + \dfrac{30e}{b} = 40 + \dfrac{20e}{b} \Rightarrow \dfrac{10e}{b} = 10
\Rightarrow \dfrac{e}{b} = 1$. So $N = 30 + 30 = 60$.

**Answer:** $60$ (b)

::: trap
On an up-escalator, walking **faster** makes you count **more** steps, not fewer — you spend less time on it, so
the escalator lifts you fewer steps and you must supply the rest. That is why $40 > 30$ here.
:::

**Q24.** Two trains are $100$ km apart on the same track, approaching each other at $50$ km/h each. A fly starts at
the nose of one train, flies at $75$ km/h to the other, instantly turns back, and keeps shuttling until the trains
meet. What total distance does the fly cover?
(a) $50$ km (b) $75$ km (c) $100$ km (d) $150$ km

**Solution:** Do **not** sum the infinite zig-zag. Find the time to collision: closing speed $= 50 + 50 = 100$
km/h over a $100$ km gap, so they meet in $1$ hour. The fly flies the entire hour at $75$ km/h, covering
$75 \times 1 = 75$ km.

**Answer:** $75$ km (b)

::: keypoint
"How far does the shuttling fly travel?" — compute the **total time** available and multiply by the fly's speed.
The infinite series is a distraction (the von Neumann anecdote).
:::
